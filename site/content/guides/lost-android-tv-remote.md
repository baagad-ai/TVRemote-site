# Lost your Android TV remote? What works before buying another

If your Android TV or Google TV is already connected to your home network, try the Google TV app on your Android phone. If the TV still remembers an old Wi-Fi network, or is waiting for first-time setup, a local-network remote app may not be able to reach it. Start with the branch that matches your screen.

## First, check whether your remote can ring

Have a Google TV Streamer and its paired Google TV Streamer Voice Remote? Briefly press the button on the back of the Streamer. The remote should sound for 30 seconds. Press any button on the remote once you find it. This button method is specific to the Streamer and its paired Streamer Voice Remote. Follow Google's [Find My Remote instructions](https://support.google.com/chromecast/answer/15322770?hl=en-GB), and don't hold the button down.

## The TV is already on your home network

Use Google's phone remote first:

1. Turn on the TV. For a separate streamer, select its HDMI input.
2. On the same home Wi-Fi, open the Google TV phone app and choose the TV from its connection button.
3. Enter the TV’s code, tap **Pair**, then try moving the TV selection.

See [Google’s pairing instructions](https://support.google.com/androidtv/answer/6122465?hl=en) if the app’s labels differ.

If the TV doesn't appear, check the simple things before changing settings: is the TV awake, and is your phone on the usual home Wi-Fi rather than mobile data or a guest network? If you can already see the TV's network status, compare it with your phone's connection.

For an unresponsive Google TV, unplug it for 10 seconds, reconnect, then retry pairing. Follow any different manufacturer instructions and let updates finish first. See [Google's virtual-remote troubleshooting](https://support.google.com/googletv/answer/15766805?hl=en).

### Both devices have internet, but they still can't connect

Internet access doesn't prove the devices can reach each other locally. Guest and managed networks may separate devices. Google identifies client isolation and sign-in-page networks as problems for its streaming devices. On a hotel, office or shared network, ask the owner which connection supports your device. Don't disable network security to make a remote app work. [Google's network guidance](https://support.google.com/chromecast/answer/7566322?hl=en-CA)

## You moved home or changed the Wi-Fi

This is the awkward loop: the TV needs a controller to join the new network, while your phone needs a reachable TV before it can act as that controller. Installing more local-network remote apps won't connect the TV to the new Wi-Fi for you.

Look for a control route that doesn't depend on that connection:

- **Buttons on the TV.** Check your model's manual for the power button or control panel and what it can operate. Some controls offer more than power; others don't. Sony's [remote troubleshooting guide](https://www.sony.com/electronics/support/articles/00030297) includes checking the TV's own buttons.
- **The television's remote, if you lost a streaming-box remote.** If HDMI-CEC control is supported and already configured, the TV remote may operate the connected device. Check both manuals. Sony calls its implementation [BRAVIA Sync](https://helpguide.sony.net/tv/nusltn1/v1/en-003/print.html); that example doesn't establish support for every TV-and-box combination.
- **A USB keyboard you already own.** Check the manufacturer's accessory guidance before trying it. Sony says many USB keyboards are recognised by its Google TV and Android TV models, while warning that functions can vary. That's a possible route on supported hardware, not a guarantee of full menu control. [Sony keyboard guidance](https://www.sony.com/electronics/support/articles/00128141)

Once one of these controls works, use the TV's network settings to join your current home Wi-Fi, then retry phone pairing. On Google TV, look under **Settings → Network & Internet**, as described in [Google's connection checks](https://support.google.com/googletv/answer/15766805?hl=en).

Ethernet may also be an option when the device has a supported port or adapter and can join your home network. For example, Google documents [Ethernet for its streaming devices](https://support.google.com/chromecast/answer/10109908?hl=en). A cable doesn't guarantee that phone pairing or unfinished setup will work. Check the exact hardware before buying an adapter.

## The TV is on a setup screen

Follow the instructions for that model and the screen you're actually seeing. Google offers [phone-assisted setup on supported Google TV devices](https://support.google.com/googletv/answer/10050221?hl=en), but its setup sequence can still require a paired remote before the phone takes over. A QR code setup option and an everyday phone remote are different stages.

Avoid a factory reset as a lost-remote shortcut. Google warns that resetting its streaming devices erases data and cannot be undone. You may end up back at setup with the same missing controller. [Google's reset warning](https://support.google.com/chromecast/answer/6254654/how-to-factory-reset-your-chromecast-android?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en-SG)

If none of the supported routes is available, borrow or buy a remote confirmed for the exact model. Check the model label on the TV or streaming box, then use the manufacturer's replacement and pairing guidance. A matching brand name alone isn't enough.

## Set up a backup while you have control

Once the TV is reachable again, pair your phone and check the controls you rely on. Keep the physical remote available for setup and any functions your phone can't operate.
