# Android TV remote app can't find your TV? Start here

Start with what you can see. A TV that never appears in the phone app needs a different check from one that pairs successfully but won't accept a search. Before changing anything, turn on the TV, check that you're looking at the right TV or streaming-device input, and confirm its network connection.

Then choose your starting point:

- **No TV listed:** check the device and its network
- **TV listed, pairing incomplete:** check the selected screen and pairing code
- **Paired, but an action fails:** test navigation separately from typing, volume, or playback

These checks focus on compatible Android TV and Google TV devices. The pairing instructions below are for Google's Google TV phone app; other apps can use different screens.

## If your TV never appears

### Check what you're trying to control

Find the TV or streaming device's model in its settings, manual, or product label, then check the manufacturer's specifications for its platform. A familiar TV brand or a working YouTube app doesn't identify the operating system.

Google documents its phone remote for Android TV and Google TV. Its separate streaming-device guide names Chromecast with Google TV and Google TV Streamer. An older Cast-only Chromecast shouldn't be treated as the same device. [Google's supported remote setup](https://support.google.com/chromecast/answer/11221499?hl=en)

If you use a streaming box, check that box's connection as well as the TV. The screen can be on while the box you're trying to control is off or showing on another input.

### Compare the actual connections

Open the phone's Wi-Fi settings and the TV's network settings. For the documented Wi-Fi setup, both should use the same network. On Google TV, Google directs you to **Settings → Network & Internet**. Menu names on other devices can differ. [Google's network check](https://support.google.com/googletv/answer/15766805?hl=en)

Check these details before blaming the app:

- Is the phone using mobile data while the TV uses home Wi-Fi?
- Did one device join a guest network?
- Does the TV still remember a network from your previous home?
- If the TV uses Ethernet, does its cable lead to the home network your phone uses? Record that wired setup when asking for help; the Wi-Fi-only instructions don't establish compatibility for every Ethernet configuration

A video loading on both devices proves they can reach the internet. It doesn't prove they can reach each other locally.

Different Wi-Fi bands aren't an automatic diagnosis. Google notes that most routers let devices on 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz communicate. Check the network arrangement before changing bands. [Google's explanation of cross-band communication](https://support.google.com/googlenest/answer/10634602?hl=en-SG)

### Know when the network needs its owner

Guest, hotel, school, and managed networks may separate connected devices. Google documents client-isolation and web-sign-in limitations for its streaming devices. If you're on someone else's network, ask its owner whether local device communication is supported. Keep router security settings intact while you establish the cause. [Google's streaming-device network guidance](https://support.google.com/chromecast/answer/7566322?hl=en-CA)

If the TV hasn't joined the network at all, restore that connection first using its original remote or a control route supported by the manufacturer.

## If the TV appears but won't pair

In the Google TV phone app, choose your TV from the TV connection button. Enter the code on that screen and tap **Pair**. Google’s Android TV guide labels it **Connect TV**. [Google's pairing instructions](https://support.google.com/androidtv/answer/6122465?hl=en)

**No code visible?** Check the selected device name against the screen in front of you. With several TVs or a plugged-in streamer, it's easy to look at the wrong display or input. Return to device selection and try the intended device again.

**Code rejected?** Use the code currently on that screen. If pairing restarts, don't keep entering a code you copied from an earlier attempt.

Seeing a device in a Cast menu or seeing media controls in Google Home isn't enough to establish that a full remote pairing has completed. Use the remote's pairing flow as your check.

## If it pairs but something doesn't work

Try moving the selection, then Back or Home. Watch whether the TV responds.

If navigation works, write down the action that fails. Test typing in the intended search field with a harmless phrase. If it fails only in one TV app, report that app and field rather than describing the entire connection as broken.

For volume, note whether sound comes from the TV, a receiver, or a soundbar. Google's guidance explains that the streaming device and external audio equipment can have separate volume settings. A working navigation control doesn't settle the audio path. [Google's volume-control guidance](https://support.google.com/chromecast/answer/10094150?hl=en)

## Try a supported restart, then collect the useful details

If the basic checks don't help, restart the TV or streamer using its official instructions. Google TV provides **Settings → System → Restart** where that menu is available. Let any active software update finish first. [Google TV restart guidance](https://support.google.com/googletv/answer/10070482?hl=en)

Afterward, reopen the phone app and retry once. For support, collect:

- Phone model, Android version, and remote-app version
- TV or streamer model and software version
- Wi-Fi or Ethernet connection, including whether it's a guest network
- The exact failure stage and which controls still work
- What changed before the problem started and what you've already tried

Leave passwords and account details out of screenshots. This is more useful than a list of resets that changed several things at once.

## Trying The Remote

The Remote is a free Android phone app for compatible Android TV and Google TV devices. It uses your home network and requires Android TV Remote Service v2; controls vary by model.

[Download the available Android APK](https://baagad-ai.github.io/TVRemote-site/#download) and check the release version and installation guidance. No signup or email is needed to download. Check compatibility before installing; a phone remote still needs a reachable TV on your home network.
