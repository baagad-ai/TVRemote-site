Phone remote keeps overshooting on your Android TV? Try one step per swipe

By The Remote · Sources reviewed 16 October 2026

If swiping on a phone remote makes the highlight on your TV jump two or three places, the app is probably turning one fast swipe into several moves. The trackpad in The Remote doesn't do that. Each swipe moves exactly one step, and you swipe and hold when you want to keep going.

Why swipe remotes overshoot

Some swipe pads read how fast or how far you swipe and send more than one move. A quick flick down a row of apps can land you past the one you wanted, and then you swipe back and overshoot the other way. It's easy to blame yourself, but it's the pad guessing.

One swipe is one step, every time

On The Remote's trackpad, a swipe moves one step up, down, left or right, however fast or far you swipe. Short swipe or long swipe, fast or slow: still one step, unless you stop and hold at the end. By default, the pad shows what each gesture sent, so you can tell it was read.

Hold to keep moving, lift to stop

For a long row or a long list, swipe and hold at the end. Keep your finger still at the end and the highlight keeps moving that way, one step at a time. Lift to stop. (If Repeat is off in Settings, each swipe is just one step.) Lift as soon as you see the right item, and you stop there.

Tap anywhere for OK

Once you're on the right item, tap anywhere on the pad for OK. There's no small target to aim for.

Back without hunting for a button

Back is often the button people miss on a phone screen. On the pad, tap with two fingers anywhere for Back, or use your phone's own back gesture. A three-finger tap is Play/Pause, and a three-finger swipe up goes Home. Swipe left or right with two fingers to skip back or forward (how far varies by app). Your phone's volume keys change the TV's volume while the pad is open.

Nothing else to hit by accident

While connected, the pad has one button: Exit, in the corner. Power is never a gesture, so a stray swipe can't turn the TV off. If you'd rather move an action to a different gesture, the Gestures screen has help and remapping, limited to the remote's own actions. The full list is in Trackpad gestures in The Remote.

If the TV drops off

The pad greys out and shows "Reconnecting to Living Room TV…", with your TV's name, so you know it isn't your swipes.

What you need

The Remote is free, with no ads and no sign-up. It runs on Android phones with Android 8.0 or later (no iPhone version). Your TV needs Android TV Remote Service v2, on the same home Wi-Fi as your phone. The trackpad works over that same TV connection, with no extra permission. Available controls vary by TV model and app. Prefer buttons? See Larger controls and Easy remote.
